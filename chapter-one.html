A Little More Light · Chapter 01 · Life & purpose

Finding the Star Within You

A reflection born on a midnight train in 2004. · By Wajid

It was the summer of 2004. I had just finished my second shift as a cashier at CVS in Manhattan and was heading home to Queens. It was one of those humid New York summer nights when the city seemed exhausted by its own existence. The midnight E train was nearly empty, making every local stop, and I sat there, alone with my thoughts, waiting to get home.

At Queens Plaza, a homeless man stepped onto the train. As the journey progressed, he slowly constructed what appeared to be a little fortress in the corner of the subway car, arranging his belongings as though carving out a small piece of the world to call his own.

Eventually, it was just the two of us.

Our eyes met. He stood up, caught my attention, and suddenly reached into his pants, rummaging for something. Instinctively, I looked away. Curiosity, perhaps mixed with fear, made me look back. He quickly pulled out his hand, revealing a small plastic package. He smiled at me, sat back down, unwrapped a croissant, and began eating.

To him, it was probably an unremarkable moment. To me, it became one of the most consequential moments of my life.

As the train continued through the darkness beneath Queens, my imagination wandered somewhere uncomfortable.

What if it hadn't been a croissant?

What if it had been a gun? What if, in that seemingly insignificant moment, my entire existence had come to an end?

What would occupy my mind in those final seconds?

Would I think of my first love? My graduation? My parents and the grief they would endure? Would I wonder what my friends would say about me? Would I think about the places I had yet to visit, the people I had yet to meet, or the person I had yet to become?

Suddenly, the things that had once seemed so important felt remarkably small. The ambitions, the possessions, the disappointments, and the little victories that had occupied so much of my young life seemed to lose their significance.

I wrestled with these thoughts for weeks, searching for an answer to a question I hadn't known I needed to ask.

Eventually, the answer came to me in five simple words:

I am not ready to die.

But what fascinated me more was why.

Was it because I feared death, or was it because I had not yet fulfilled the purpose of my existence?

That was when I began developing a philosophy I would come to call Finding the Star Within You.

Our debt to the universe

Consider, for a moment, the extraordinary improbability of your existence.

Of all the possibilities across the vast history of the universe, you came into existence at a particular moment in time. Not a century earlier, not a generation later. You arrived when you did, into a particular set of circumstances, surrounded by people whose lives would inevitably intersect with yours.

I like to believe there is something meaningful about that.

Perhaps we are not simply here to consume the experiences life offers us, but to contribute something to the existence that made those experiences possible.

Perhaps our very existence creates an obligation. A debt to the universe, not one to be repaid in wealth, accomplishments, or recognition, but through what we leave behind in others.

Are we all destined for greatness?

I don't believe so.

We are not all destined to be great. We are all capable of contributing to something greater than ourselves.

Much like the smallest particles that come together to form an atom, each of us contributes to a reality far larger than our individual existence.

Some of us become parents, others become mentors. Some lead nations, others quietly keep the world functioning. Some spend their lives exploring distant lands, while others never leave the neighborhood in which they were born. Some inherit abundance; others inherit adversity.

Life does not distribute its privileges, opportunities, or hardships equally. Yet the value of a human being cannot be measured solely by the circumstances they inherit or the position they eventually occupy.

A janitor can teach a lesson in dignity that a billionaire may spend a lifetime trying to understand. A stranger can offer a kindness that changes the course of someone's life. A person whose name never appears in a history book can become the most important chapter in someone else's story.

Perhaps the purpose of life is not to compete for the most significant role, but to bring significance to whatever role we find ourselves in.

The light we leave behind

I have always been fascinated by the stars.

There are billions upon billions of them scattered across the cosmos. Yet some of the light that reaches our eyes tonight began its journey long before we were born. Some of those stars may no longer even exist as we see them, and still, their light travels through the darkness.

Their existence has ended, but their influence has not.

I believe human beings possess a similar capacity.

We spend so much of our lives trying to become something. We pursue education, careers, wealth, influence, love, and recognition. We climb mountains of our own making, believing that reaching the summit will somehow validate our existence.

But perhaps the true measure of reaching our highest potential is not how brightly we shine, but how much light we give away.

What good is wisdom if it is never shared? What good is success if it never creates an opportunity for someone else? What good is strength if it is never used to lift another person?

Billions of people have walked this earth before us. History remembers relatively few of them by name. But remembrance is not always written in books, carved into monuments, or celebrated by the masses.

Sometimes, it exists in the quiet gratitude of a single human being.

In the confidence of someone you believed in when nobody else did. In the courage of someone you encouraged when they wanted to give up. In the kindness someone extends to a stranger because, years earlier, you extended that same kindness to them.

That is how our light continues to travel.

You do not need to change the entire world to justify your existence. Sometimes, changing someone's world is enough.

When the train reaches its final stop

More than two decades have passed since that humid summer night in New York.

I often wonder whether that man ever realized what he had given me. He was simply eating a croissant on a midnight train, perhaps trying to find a little comfort in a world that had not always been kind to him.

Yet without knowing my name, without speaking a word, and without intending to teach me anything, he changed the way I thought about life.

Perhaps that, in itself, is the most beautiful illustration of this philosophy.

We rarely know how far our light travels.

One day, each of us will arrive at our final moment. The titles we earned will no longer matter. The money we accumulated will belong to someone else. The possessions we cherished will be scattered, and the world will continue moving without us, much like that midnight train.

I hope that when my moment comes, I will not be consumed by the things I failed to acquire, but comforted by the things I managed to give.

And if somewhere in this vast world, even one person can look back on their life and say,

I am grateful that our paths crossed. My life is a little better because you existed.

Then perhaps I will have repaid my debt to the universe.

Perhaps I will finally understand that the purpose of finding the star within me was never to become the brightest star in the sky.

It was to leave behind a little more light than I found.

And when the time comes to meet my maker, I hope I can answer that same question I asked myself on a midnight train in 2004, only this time, with a different answer.

I am ready.

My light will travel on.
